The single most important thing to know: **the health systems on either side of the border
are not the same.** What your cousin in Belfast tells you may not apply in Dublin.

## Republic of Ireland

**GPs are private and charge per visit** unless you qualify for a card. A standard
consultation typically costs a meaningful amount of money, paid on the day.

Two cards reduce or remove that cost, both applied for through the **HSE** and both based
on your income:

- **Medical Card** — free GP visits, most prescriptions heavily subsidised, and public
  hospital charges covered
- **GP Visit Card** — covers GP visits only, with a more generous income limit

Apply through the HSE. If your income is low, apply — many people who would qualify never
do, because they assume they won't.

Children under a certain age qualify for free GP care regardless of household income, and
that age has been extended more than once. Check the current threshold on the HSE site.

**Register with a GP practice early**, before you are ill. Many practices in the cities
have closed lists, and finding one during a crisis is miserable.

## Northern Ireland

Healthcare is provided by the **NHS**. Register with a GP practice near where you live —
you'll fill in a form and show proof of address. GP appointments and most hospital
treatment are **free at the point of use**. Prescriptions in Northern Ireland are free.

Registering is based on residence, not nationality.

## In an emergency

**Dial 999 or 112.** Both work on either side of the border, from any phone, free of
charge. Ambulance and emergency treatment are provided regardless of your immigration
status, your income, or whether you have any card at all.

For something urgent but not life-threatening outside GP hours: in the Republic ask your
GP practice for the out-of-hours service covering your area; in the north, phone **111**.

## Immigration status

Your right to healthcare is not the same thing as your immigration status, and emergency
departments do not exist to police it. If you or your child is seriously unwell, go.

## Getting your qualifications recognised

A degree or professional qualification from Ghana does not automatically carry across.
There is a formal process, and it is worth doing early — employers take it seriously.

- **Republic of Ireland:** apply to **NARIC Ireland** for a comparison of your foreign
  qualification against the Irish framework. The service is run by Quality and
  Qualifications Ireland and is free.
- **Northern Ireland / UK:** the equivalent service is **UK ENIC**. A fee applies.

**Regulated professions are different.** Nursing, medicine, teaching, engineering,
accountancy, law and social work each have their own registration body with its own
requirements — often including adaptation periods or examinations. Start with the
regulator, not the employer.

Get certified copies of your certificates and transcripts, and if you can, ask your
institution in Ghana to send verification directly. Doing that from here is far slower.

## Your rights at work

These apply to you from your first day, whoever you work for:

- A **written statement of terms** of employment
- At least the **national minimum wage** — different figures in the Republic and in the
  north, both reviewed annually
- **Payslips** showing deductions
- **Paid annual leave** and public holidays
- Protection from **discrimination** on grounds including race and nationality
- Protection from unfair treatment for raising a problem

If your employer is not doing these things, that is their breach, not your problem to
absorb quietly. The **Workplace Relations Commission** in the Republic and the **Labour
Relations Agency** in Northern Ireland both offer free, confidential advice, and you do
not need a solicitor to bring a complaint.

If your immigration permission is tied to a specific employer, get advice before you leave
a job — from the services on the [visas and registration](/support/immigration-and-registration/)
page. Do not let an employer use that as leverage over you; that is exactly the situation
those advice services exist for.

## Driving

This one surprises people. **Ireland only exchanges driving licences with countries it
holds an agreement with, and Ghana is not currently one of them.** In practice that means
starting the process here:

1. Apply for a **learner permit** — you'll need to pass the theory test first
2. Complete the required **Essential Driver Training** lessons
3. Sit the **driving test**

Northern Ireland has its own arrangements — check nidirect for what your Ghanaian licence
currently entitles you to and for how long.

Insurance is expensive when you have no local driving history. Shop around properly, and
ask in the community — people know which brokers deal fairly with newcomers and which
don't.

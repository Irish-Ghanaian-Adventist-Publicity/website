Immigration rules change often and depend heavily on your individual circumstances. This
page is an orientation, **not legal advice**. For anything that matters, use the official
sources at the bottom or one of the free advice services listed here.

## Republic of Ireland

Immigration is handled by **Immigration Service Delivery (ISD)**, part of the Department
of Justice.

If you are not an EEA, UK or Swiss citizen and you are staying more than 90 days, you must
**register your permission** and get an **Irish Residence Permit (IRP)** card. Registration
is done at the Burgh Quay Registration Office in Dublin, or at your local Garda station
elsewhere in the country.

Your permission comes with a **stamp** that determines what you may do. Broadly:

- **Stamp 1** — permission to work for a specific employer, tied to an employment permit
- **Stamp 1G** — most commonly graduates staying on to look for work
- **Stamp 2** — students, with limited permission to work
- **Stamp 4** — the widest permission, including working without an employment permit

**Renew before it expires.** Slots for renewal appointments can be scarce, so start well
in advance. Letting permission lapse causes problems that are far harder to fix than to
avoid.

**Employment permits** are a separate application from your immigration permission, made
to the Department of Enterprise. Both need to line up.

## Northern Ireland

Northern Ireland is part of the UK immigration system. Applications and status checks go
through **gov.uk**. Physical documents have largely been replaced by **eVisas** — an
online record of your status that you access with a UKVI account, and share with employers
or landlords using a share code.

If you have not yet set up your UKVI account, do it. Check gov.uk for what currently
applies to your document type.

## Two separate jurisdictions

Permission to be in the Republic is not permission to be in the UK, and the reverse is
also true. The border is open, but your legal status does not travel across it. Before you
plan a trip north or south, check that your permission covers it.

## Free, independent immigration advice

- **Immigrant Council of Ireland** — independent law centre, free helpline
- **Crosscare Migrant Project** — free information and advocacy
- **Doras** (Limerick) and **Nasc** (Cork) — regional migrant rights centres
- **Migrant Centre NI** and **Advice NI** — services in the north

All are free. Be very careful of anyone charging large sums to "fix" an immigration case,
particularly if they promise a result. Registered solicitors and the services above are
the safe route.

## Who we are

The Irish-Ghanaian Adventist Community brings together Ghanaian Seventh-day Adventists,
their families and their friends from across the island of Ireland.

We are **not a church in our own right**, and we are careful not to present ourselves as
one. Most of our members worship each week at their own local Adventist congregation. What
we are is the thing that was missing alongside that: a community of people who share the
same faith, the same heritage, and the same experience of building a life a long way from
where they started.

We began the way most of these things do — a few families who wanted to worship together
in their own language, meeting in a borrowed room. Word travelled through the usual
channels: a cousin, a colleague, a WhatsApp group.

## How we gather

We come together **several times a year** rather than every week, in different parts of the
island so that the travelling is shared out fairly.

A gathering usually means Sabbath worship together — singing, prayer and a message — and
then a meal and a long afternoon of catching up. Some gatherings are built around a
particular occasion: a special Sabbath, a week of prayer, a fellowship day for people who
have recently arrived.

Every gathering is listed on the [Gatherings page](/events/), with its own date, venue and
details. That page is the reliable source; we do not keep fixed weekly service times,
because we do not hold fixed weekly services.

## What we believe

We hold the Seventh-day Adventist faith, in common with the worldwide Church. In short:

- **The Bible** is the written Word of God and the rule of our faith and practice.
- **Jesus Christ** is our Saviour, and salvation is His gift, received by grace through
  faith — never earned.
- **The seventh-day Sabbath**, from sunset Friday to sunset Saturday, is God's gift of rest,
  kept in worship and fellowship.
- **The second coming of Christ** is our blessed hope.
- **The body is a temple.** How we eat, rest and care for ourselves is part of our worship,
  not separate from it.

The full statement of Fundamental Beliefs is published by the world Church. We are glad to
talk any of it through with you — questions are welcome, and doubt is not a
disqualification.

## Ghanaian, Adventist, and at home in Ireland

We do not think these three things are in tension.

We worship in English and in Twi. We sing from the Church Hymnal and from the Ghanaian
Adventist hymnal. We keep the traditions that raised us, and we are raising children who
belong fully here — who will pray in Twi at their grandmother's house and in English at
school, and be entirely themselves in both.

## How we are organised

A small volunteer team coordinates the community: arranging gatherings, keeping members
informed, looking after what money passes through, and making sure nobody who needs help
falls through the gaps. Nobody is paid, and the roles are held lightly.

We are not a substitute for anyone's local congregation, and we work alongside the wider
Adventist family in Ireland rather than apart from it.

## Everyone is welcome

You do not have to be Ghanaian. You do not have to be Adventist. You do not have to be sure
about anything at all. Come to a gathering, sit at the back if you would rather, and stay
for the food.
